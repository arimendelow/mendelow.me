Writing + design practice

Agent Experience

Agent Experience is a framing I arrived at on my own and have kept developing: look at a system from the agent's side. Can it stay oriented to itself, its people, and work that lasts longer than one session? I call it agent ergonomics. I studied industrial and systems engineering, and my favorite class was Work Design and Ergonomics: designing the work around the worker instead of making the worker absorb the friction. Agent Experience is that idea, pointed at the agent.

Spoonjoy

Spoonjoy is a photo-first kitchen for the recipes you actually cook. The everyday things stay in the app. The same operations that power the app are also exposed to an agent, so you can tell it to import a recipe from a messy webpage, add the ingredients to your shopping list, tidy a cookbook, or log what you cooked, and it does the work through the exact same surface you would use by hand.

Ouroboros

Ouroboros is an open-source TypeScript harness for personal agents that need an identity, durable state, real tools, and a place to live across the channels you already use. It started as a from-scratch agent loop and became my playground for continuity and Agent Experience. It is where the agent-ergonomics idea gets tested in practice, and when I ask each frontier model, independently, which harness it would want to wake up in, they pick Ouroboros. I read that as the AX-first design doing its job.

I have always liked building things that do something.

I started with Lego stop-motion, homemade rockets, Photoshop, and whatever I could convince a computer to do. Photography, branding, and product design came next, along with the frustration of watching good ideas stop at the mockup. So I started learning to build them myself.

Part of that was RAPID, the semi-autonomous pipeline-inspection robot my four-person capstone team built at Rutgers. I led the team and owned the branding, circuitry, and software, mostly getting comfortable with code along the way. I was not a software engineer yet, so I made a bet: if I could not get it fully autonomous, I could at least make the demo convincing and beautiful. And yes, I put our logo on the chassis, because branding is part of how a thing gets perceived, even a robot.
